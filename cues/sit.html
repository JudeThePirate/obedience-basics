Sit

Description

The cue word 'Sit' prompts the dog to move into a sitting position, either while the dog is in motion or from a standing position. The cue word can be supplemented or replaced by the Sit hand signal. If the dog is lying down, the cue 'Up Sit' should be used instead of 'Sit', and will result in the dog also moving into a sitting position.

It is not necessary for the dog to return to the heel position before moving into a sitting position when given the Sit cue - the dog should sit wherever they are located. If the handler wants the dog to move into a sitting position at heel (beside their left leg) then a 'Return to Heel' or 'Come Around' cue should be used prior to giving the 'Sit' Cue.

Hand signal

The hand is flat with the palm facing upwards with the fingers together and pointing towards the dog.

The hand moves upwards, from approximately waist height, in front of the dog's nose.

Equipment

  • Lead
  • Kibble

Training prompts

Puppies are typically confident performing Sit by 8 weeks. The cue can be taught with or without a food lure, or simply captured and marked by a vigilant handler.

To train the behaviour with a food lure, hold a piece of kibble in the fingertips immediately in front of the dog's nose, and slowly raise the kibble in an upward direction, keeping the kibble close to the dog's nose. As the dog lift's it's nose to follow the piece of kibble, it will move into a sitting position. Mark the moment the dog completes the behaviour and release the kibble.

Where the dog has already an established training history, or once the dog has established a clear understanding of the cue word, the same process can be followed using air-kibble in the fingertips, with food delivery occurring after the behaviour has been marked. The final stage will involve substituting the pinched fingers holding air-kibble with the open-flat hand Sit hand signal.

Like all behaviours, the Sit cue can alternatively be taught by capturing the Sit behaviour ("Yes, Sit", followed by a food reward) at any time when it is voluntarily completed by the dog.
