Stop

Description

The cue word 'Stop' prompts the dog to stop moving while the dog is in motion and remain standing. The 'Up Stand' cue should be used if the dog is sitting or lying down.

It is not necessary for the dog to return to the heel position when given the Stop cue - the dog should stop wherever they are located. If the handler wants the dog to move into a standing position at heel (beside their left leg) then a 'Return to Heel' or 'Come Around' cue should be used instead.

Equipment

  • Lead
  • Kibble

Training prompts

The cue can be taught with or without a food lure, or simply captured and marked by a vigilant handler.

To train the behaviour with a food lure, hold a piece of kibble in the fingertips immediately in front of the dog's nose, and slowly move the kibble away from the dog's nose. The dog will move into a standing position as it follows the kibble. Mark the moment the dog completes the behaviour and release the kibble.

Where the dog has already an established training history, or once the dog has established a clear understanding of the cue word, the same process can be followed using air-kibble in the fingertips, with food delivery occurring after the behaviour has been marked. The final stage will involve using the Stop cue with no hand signal, and marking then rewarding the behaviour when it is performed correctly.

Like all behaviours, the cue can alternatively be taught by capturing the Stop behaviour ("Yes, Stop", followed by a food reward) at any time when it is voluntarily completed by the dog.
